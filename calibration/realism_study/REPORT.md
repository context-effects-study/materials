# The realism comparison: the published pages rated on the same question

The invented categories missed the realism threshold of 5.0 in both waves that measured it (means of 4.53 to 4.77 in
wave 2 and of 4.36 to 4.68 in wave 3). We had set the threshold before anyone had rated the page of a real product
on the question. We therefore asked whether the invented categories or the threshold was the problem. In this study,
112 new raters, none of whom took part in a calibration wave, rated the pages of the seven published categories on
the same 7-point question ("does this read like a real product page?"). We showed the pages exactly as wave 3 showed
the invented ones. We added the study after the wave-3 data, to interpret the realism ratings, and we report it as
such. `README.md` gives the record of the study, and `results/realism_results.json` gives every number below.

## What we found

- The published categories scored 4.17 to 5.05 on the same question (pooled mean 4.70, 95 percent interval 4.49 to
  4.91). Six of the seven scored below 5.0, and only the backpacks (5.05) scored above it. The earbuds, a real
  product, scored 4.17, lower than all three invented categories.
- The invented categories (4.36, 4.53 and 4.68) lie inside the range of the published categories. The pooled mean
  of the published categories minus that of the invented categories is 0.18 (95 percent interval -0.07 to 0.42), so
  we cannot distinguish the difference from zero. Of the raters of published pages, 45 percent rated a page 5 or
  higher, against 44 percent of the raters of invented pages.
- The raters of the published pages made the same complaints as the raters of the invented pages: a plain page with
  two attributes shows no photos and no full specifications.

## What we conclude

We conclude that a threshold of 5.0 was too high for the plain page with two attributes that every category uses,
and we report the realism of the invented pages as similar to that of the published pages. We do not change the
threshold after the fact. One category is an exception: the electric kettles scored 0.34 points below the pooled
mean of the published categories (95 percent interval 0.08 to 0.60), although they still scored above the lowest
published category.
