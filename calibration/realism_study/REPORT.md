# The realism comparison: published pages on the same question

After the invented categories missed the 5.0 realism threshold in both waves that measured it
(4.53 to 4.77 in wave 2; 4.36 to 4.68 in wave 3), one question remained: is that a defect of the
invented categories, or of the threshold? The threshold had been set before any real product
page had ever been rated on the item. So 112 fresh raters (none from any calibration wave)
scored the seven PUBLISHED categories' pages on the identical 7-point "reads like a real product
page" question, with the pages rendered exactly as wave 3 rendered the invented ones. This study
is a disclosed post-registration amendment to interpretation; the launch record is
`../launch_records/ANCHOR_LAUNCH_RECORD.md` and the results file is
`../anchor_data/anchor_results.json`.

What it found:

- Published, real categories score 4.17 to 5.05 on the same item (pooled 4.70, 95 percent
  interval [4.49, 4.91]). Six of the seven miss the 5.0 threshold; only backpacks (5.05) clears
  it, barely. Real earbuds pages scored 4.17, LOWER than all three invented categories.
- The invented categories (4.36 / 4.53 / 4.68) sit entirely inside the published range. The
  pooled published-minus-invented difference is +0.18, 95 percent interval [-0.07, +0.42], not
  distinguishable from zero; the share of raters answering 5 or higher is 45 versus 44 percent.
- The raters' complaints about the PUBLISHED pages reproduce the ones the invented pages drew:
  the stripped two-attribute format shows no photos or specifications.

Reading: the 5.0 threshold was miscalibrated for the plain two-attribute page format every
category shares, and the protocol reports realism at parity with published pages rather than as
a failure. The threshold is not revised retroactively. One honest caveat: the kettles category
alone sits 0.34 points below the published pooled mean (interval [0.08, 0.60]), though still
above the lowest published category.
